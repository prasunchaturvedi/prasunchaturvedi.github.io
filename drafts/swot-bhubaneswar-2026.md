# Bhubaneswar as an IT destination: a SWOT for 2026

*Draft, not for publication. Revisits my February 2024 SWOT. Prasun Chaturvedi.*

In February 2024 I set out a SWOT of Bhubaneswar as a location for IT and IT-enabled services. Nearly three years on, the policy landscape has changed more than the ground reality. The state government has issued a run of sector policies in 2025, and two semiconductor units have been approved for the city. The question I keep returning to is whether policy intent is now translating into jobs at scale. My assessment follows.

## Strengths

**A complete policy stack.** Within a single year the state put in place an AI policy, a new IT policy, an electronics component manufacturing policy and a Global Capability Centre (GCC) policy. The [Odisha AI Policy 2025](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-adopts-ai-policy-2025-to-stimulate-innovation-inclusive-growth/amp), approved in May 2025, rests on four pillars (infrastructure, skills, energy and regulation) and sets up an Odisha AI Mission with a high-level taskforce. The [IT Policy 2025](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-it-policy-2025-gets-cabinet-nod-to-boost-digital-growth-innovation/), cleared by the cabinet in August 2025, targets one million direct and indirect jobs in IT and ITeS. Few states offer this breadth of aligned instruments.

**Unusually generous capital support.** The IT Policy offers a [30% capital subsidy with no investment ceiling](https://odishatv.in/news/odisha/odisha-cabinet-clears-new-it-electronics-policies-now-concessions-for-projects-over-rs-500-cr-270801), along with rental and land support, SGST reimbursement and utility waivers. Projects above Rs 500 crore or employing over 1,000 people qualify for tailored packages. The [Electronics Component Manufacturing Policy 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/odisha-govt-approves-new-it-electronics-component-manufacturing-policies-125082501283_1.html), approved alongside, offers 50% capital subsidy to the first ten large projects and 35% thereafter.

**An emerging semiconductor anchor.** In August 2025 the Union Cabinet [approved two semiconductor units in Info Valley, Bhubaneswar](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-semiconductor-manufacturing-units-in-odisha-punjab-and-andhra-pradesh-with-an-outlay-of-rs-4600-crore): a silicon carbide compound semiconductor fab described as the country's first commercial one, and an advanced glass-substrate packaging unit. Ground was [subsequently broken on the packaging unit](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/odisha-breaks-ground-on-3d-glass-solutions-advanced-glass-chip-packaging-unit-state-eyes-top-tier-semiconductor-hub-status/) [verify date]. This gives Bhubaneswar a hardware story that most tier-2 IT cities lack.

**Steady growth from a modest base.** The state's Economic Survey 2024-25 reports [software and electronics exports of about Rs 12,900 crore in 2023-24](https://sambadenglish.com/latest-news/software-electronics-exports-from-odisha-crossed-rs-12000-crore-in-2023-24-economic-survey-8727000), up from Rs 4,500 crore in 2019-20. The state's Electronics and IT Department counts [over 300 IT companies in the Bhubaneswar-Jatni-Khordha belt](https://sambadenglish.com/latest-news/bhubaneswar-emerging-as-major-it-hub-with-over-70-companies-in-last-7-years-10543230), more than 70 of which arrived in the last seven years.

## Weaknesses

**Scale remains small against the target.** The same department puts current employment in that belt at roughly 35,000 professionals. Against a one-million-job ambition, the gap is close to thirty-fold. Even counting indirect jobs, the target implies a growth rate the city has not yet shown.

**Salary and opportunity gap.** At a gathering of Odia IT professionals in December 2025, speakers [pointed to limited local opportunities and lower salary offers](https://www.orissapost.com/odia-it-professionals-decry-lack-of-opportunities/) as reasons experienced talent stays outside the state. This was my central concern in 2024 and it has not materially changed.

**Data quality.** Official figures do not always reconcile. The Economic Survey export number for 2023-24 differs from a [separate estimate of about Rs 7,500 crore given in the Assembly](https://sambadenglish.com/latest-news/software-electronics-exports-from-odisha-crossed-rs-12000-crore-in-2023-24-economic-survey-8727000) for the same year. Investors and researchers need one consistent, published series.

## Opportunities

**Global Capability Centres.** The [GCC Policy 2025](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-to-have-gcc-policy-2025-to-attract-rs-1000-cr-investments-create-50000-jobs/), approved in November 2025, aims for at least five GCC hubs, over Rs 1,000 crore of investment and more than 50,000 jobs, across the Bhubaneswar-Cuttack-Puri-Paradip and Bargarh-Jharsuguda-Sambalpur corridors. GCCs value cost, stability and talent retention over metro brand. Bhubaneswar can compete on all three if it can show a deep enough mid-career talent pool.

**Returning talent.** Roughly [80% of the 27,000-plus IT hires in the city between 2018 and 2024 were from Odisha](https://sambadenglish.com/latest-news/bhubaneswar-emerging-as-major-it-hub-with-over-70-companies-in-last-7-years-10543230). The diaspora of Odia professionals is a recruitment channel that GCCs and product firms can target directly, provided roles carry comparable pay and seniority.

**Applied AI, cyber security and public digital infrastructure.** The IT Policy names cyber security, AI, cloud and data analytics as priorities, and the AI Policy points to governance, agriculture, health and disaster management as use cases. Odisha's experience in digital delivery of public services and disaster management offers a domain base for specialised firms, not only generic services.

**Hardware-software convergence.** Semiconductor fabrication and packaging bring demand for design, test, embedded software and supply-chain IT. The IT Policy also provides for a [cable landing station](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-it-policy-2025-gets-cabinet-nod-to-boost-digital-growth-innovation/), which would strengthen the case for data centres. Graduate pipelines such as [IIT Bhubaneswar's placement season](https://www.iitbbs.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Media-Coverage-500-placements-at-IIT-Bhubaneswar-in-the-2025-26-season-1.pdf) (about 500 placements in 2025-26 [verify]) can feed this demand locally.

## Threats

**Incentive competition.** Most large states now run their own IT, GCC and electronics policies. Subsidies attract a first decision; they rarely sustain a second expansion. Bhubaneswar has to win on ecosystem depth, not only on incentive rates.

**AI compressing entry-level work.** Much of the city's growth has come from delivery and support roles that generative AI is already automating. A one-million-job target set against shrinking entry-level demand needs a deliberate shift towards higher-value work.

**Execution lag.** Policies are approved; land allotment, plug-and-play space, airport connectivity and single-window clearance decide outcomes. Professionals themselves have [asked for faster land and single-window support](https://www.orissapost.com/odia-it-professionals-decry-lack-of-opportunities/). Any slippage here hands the advantage to established hubs.

**Climate exposure.** Coastal cyclone risk is a standing factor in site selection for data centres and GCCs. Odisha's disaster management record is a strength, but business continuity planning will feature in every investor's due diligence.

## My assessment

Compared with 2024, Bhubaneswar's strengths are now on paper as well as in potential. The policy stack is among the most complete of any tier-2 IT city in India, and the semiconductor approvals change the city's profile. What has not yet moved is the mid-career talent market and the absolute scale of employment. The next two years should be measured on three indicators: GCC commitments converted to operating centres, median salary parity with comparable cities, and a single, consistent export and employment series published by the state. I will revisit this assessment against those indicators.
