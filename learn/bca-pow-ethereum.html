Difficulty, target and hash rate

Condition: H(header) < target. A smaller target means fewer valid hashes and higher difficulty. Difficulty is a PoW concept; PoS has none.

Block discovery is a Poisson process: the 10-minute Bitcoin interval is an average, not a timer. When hash rate rises, blocks come faster and the next retarget lowers the target; when it falls, the target rises.

Worked example

Target interval 10 min at 100 TH/s. Hash rate doubles to 200 TH/s, so blocks arrive about every 5 min until the retarget (every 2,016 blocks in Bitcoin), which halves the target (doubles difficulty) and restores 10 min.

High difficulty alone is not security: pool concentration, ASIC supply and network propagation also govern resistance to 51% and selfish-mining attacks.

Energy and misleading metrics

Energy use is driven by miner participation, ASIC efficiency (J/TH), reward and fee revenue, and electricity prices, plus cooling and e-waste. Energy (TWh) and carbon intensity (gCO2/kWh) are separate: the same megawatts on hydro and on coal differ greatly.

“kWh per transaction” misleads: it is an average, not a marginal cost — miners hash headers whether a block holds 1 or 3,000 transactions — and batching or layer-2 channels settle many payments in one on-chain transaction.

The Merge (15 September 2022)

Ethereum’s execution layer joined the PoS Beacon Chain. Each node now runs an execution client (Geth, Nethermind: EVM, state, mempool) and a consensus client (Prysm, Lighthouse: proposals, attestations, fork choice, finality). Consensus energy fell by more than 99.9% with full history preserved.

Validators, slots and epochs

  • Validator: deposits 32 ETH; duties are block proposal and attestation.
  • Slot: 12 s; one pseudo-randomly chosen proposer (the slot stays empty if it is offline).
  • Attestations: committee votes on the head and checkpoints; each validator attests once per epoch.
  • Epoch: 32 slots = 6.4 min.
  • Checkpoint: ≥ 2/3 of stake attesting makes it justified; when the next checkpoint is justified on top, the earlier one is finalized (about 2 epochs, ~13 min).

LMD-GHOST picks the head by latest attestation weight; Casper FFG finalizes checkpoints — reverting one requires at least 1/3 of stake to be slashed. Confirmation (in a block) < fork-choice head < finality: progressively stronger guarantees.

Penalties, slashing and risks

Inactivity penaltySlashing
TriggerOffline, missed dutiesProvable equivocation: two blocks for one slot, double or surround votes
EffectMissed rewards, small leakStake partly burned; forced exit

Risks: stolen validator keys; correlated bugs if one client exceeds 2/3 of validators (hence client diversity); stake and cloud concentration; and smart-contract bugs, which consensus does not protect against. Operators running the same key on two machines as “backup” have been slashed for double signing.
